### Course Overview

**CS 6501 / CS 4501: Learning for Interactive Robots**  
**Term**: Fall 2026  
**Institution**: University of Virginia (UVA) &middot; Department of Computer Science  
**Lab**: [LIVE Lab (Learning for Interactive, Vision, and Embodied AI)](https://live-robotics-uva.github.io)  
**Instructor**: Prof. Yen-Ling Kuo  

Interactive robots must perceive their surrounding environment, anticipate human intent, interpret verbal and non-verbal cues, adapt to individual preferences, and interact safely and fluently in shared workspaces. This course explores cutting-edge machine learning and embodied AI algorithms that enable robots to learn from, collaborate with, and adapt to humans.

---

### Course Topics & Scope

Throughout the semester, we investigate the fundamental paradigms bridging perception, learning, and physical action:
- **Foundation Models for Robotics**: Vision models, multimodal LLMs, and foundation models for action (VLA).
- **Grounded Language & Commonsense**: Connecting semantic representations to physical embodiment and spatial contexts.
- **World Models & Policy Generation**: Generative policy architectures, diffusion-based trajectory generation, and code-as-policies.
- **Interactive Feedback & Corrections**: Physical interventions, language feedback, and corrective demonstration learning.
- **Human Modeling & Intent Prediction**: Non-verbal communication, intent estimation, gaze, and trajectory forecasting.
- **Preference Elicitation & Shared Autonomy**: Reward learning from human feedback (RLHF), assistive teleoperation, and dynamic control allocation.
- **Continual Learning & Safety**: Multi-task learning, distribution shifts, safety guarantees, and human trust calibration.

### Why Lecture Reports?

Following the inspiration of the [ICLR Blogposts Track](https://iclr-blogposts.github.io/2026/about/), this course emphasizes scientific communication, synthesis, and critical thinking.

Rather than static lecture slides or private homework write-ups, student teams produce public, Distill-style blog posts summarizing and critiquing the assigned readings and classroom discussions for each lecture:
- **Intuitive Explanations**: Unpacking complex proofs, architectural mechanics, and algorithms with clear exposition.
- **Interactive Visualizations**: Interactive figures and diagrams illustrating robotic state spaces, control loops, and learning dynamics.
- **Critical Discourse**: Identifying unstated assumptions, failure modes, reproducibility challenges, and promising future research directions.
- **Open Peer Review**: Submissions are refined through pull request reviews by peers and course instructors prior to publication.
